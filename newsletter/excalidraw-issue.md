# 💡 **Quick Win: Excalidraw**

**The Problem:** A diagram is the fastest way to explain a process and the slowest thing to make. The tools most of us have open — PowerPoint, Word, Google Slides — were built for slides, not for flows. So the work isn't thinking about the process. It's fighting connectors that won't stay attached, shapes that snap to the wrong guide, and text boxes that resize themselves. The diagram is a ten-minute idea trapped behind forty minutes of fiddling, so most of the time we give up and send the paragraph instead.

**The Solution:** Two pieces that fit together. First, you describe the process to _Claude_ or _ChatGPT_ and ask for it as **Mermaid** — a plain-text way of writing a flowchart, which the AI writes for you so you never have to learn it. Then you paste that text into _Excalidraw_, a free online whiteboard, and it draws the entire diagram. Boxes, arrows, layout, spacing. From there you edit it like any normal drawing.

Excalidraw opens in your browser, costs nothing, and needs no account.

**How to do it:**

1. **Describe the process to the AI:** Explain the flow the way you'd explain it to a new colleague, then ask:

“_Turn this process into a Mermaid flowchart. Use short labels, show the decision points as diamonds, and give me only the code block._“ —

2. **Open Excalidraw:** Go to [excalidraw.com](https://excalidraw.com/). It opens straight onto a blank canvas. Nothing to install, nothing to sign up for.

3. **Paste the text:** Open the menu (the icon in the top-left corner) and look for **Mermaid to Excalidraw**. Paste in the code the AI gave you. The diagram appears in the preview on the right. Click **Insert**.

4. **Make it yours:** Everything on the canvas is now an ordinary shape. Drag boxes, retype labels, change colours, delete the step that turned out to be wrong. The arrows stay attached when you move things — the part PowerPoint never gets right.

5. **Get it out:** Right-click and **Copy to clipboard as PNG** to paste it straight into an email, a Word document or a slide. Or use **Export image** if you want a PNG or SVG file to keep.

**The Result:**

1. A finished process diagram in about three minutes instead of forty.

2. Something you can actually edit next month when the process changes, instead of rebuilding it.

3. A picture your colleagues will read, instead of a paragraph they'll skim.

🚀 **Pro Tips to get even more out of it:**

1. **The hand-drawn look is doing a job.** Excalidraw diagrams come out deliberately sketchy, like something drawn on a napkin. Keep it that way for anything you want feedback on. A polished diagram gets "looks good"; a rough one gets people telling you which step is wrong.

2. **Feed it last week's transcript.** Remember the meeting-transcript trick from the previous issue? Paste the same transcript in and ask for the Mermaid flowchart instead. One recorded call, one written procedure, one diagram, and nothing rewatched.

3. **Sketch together on a call.** The **Share** button creates a live link. Whoever you send it to joins in their browser with no account, and you both draw on the same canvas. The session is end-to-end encrypted, so it's fine for internal work.

4. **Ask the AI for the diagram you didn't think of.** The same description will give you a sequence diagram, an org chart or a timeline if you ask for one. Try "show this as a swimlane so it's clear who does what" when responsibilities are the confusing part.

⏱️ **Time to set up:** 0 minutes. It's a website — the diagram itself takes about 3.
💰 **Time you'll save:** 30–45 minutes per diagram

❗**Quick reminder:** Excalidraw keeps your canvas in your browser, not in an account. That's good for privacy and bad for memory — clearing your browsing data will wipe a drawing you never saved. Use **Save to file** to keep the `.excalidraw` version, which is the editable original. The PNG is just a picture of it.
